GENERAL INFORMATION

Title: Wastewater treatment plants of Switzerland: catchments, plant locations and
  discharge points, 2019 to 2025

Short name: ARA_WWTP_2019_2025

Description:
  Yearly spatial datasets of Swiss wastewater treatment plants (Abwasserreinigungsanlagen,
  ARA; wastewater treatment plants, WWTP) for 2019 to 2025. Each year is described by three
  layers: the catchment (Einzugsgebiet, EZG) as polygons, the plant location (Standort) as
  points, and the discharge point into the receiving water (Einleitstelle) as points. Seven
  years times three layers gives 21 datasets.
  The dataset was built by updating the Eawag ARA_2014 dataset, which itself revised Maurer
  and Herlyn (2007). Attribute information was collected and compiled by Rebekka Gulde,
  Yannick Burkard and Katia Soland at Eawag in summer 2026 from several sources, including
  geodienste.ch, the VSA database, and material from cantons and municipalities. The spatial
  data was updated accordingly by Katia Soland.
  What this dataset is not: it is not an official federal product. Collecting cantonal
  wastewater data is the responsibility of the Federal Office for the Environment (FOEN).
  Because not all cantons have published their data, that collection has not been updated
  consistently since 2014. Eawag researchers needed more recent figures, so this dataset was
  produced as an interim, best-effort reconstruction to meet that need. Where it disagrees
  with a cantonal or federal source, the other source is more likely to be authoritative.

METHODOLOGICAL INFORMATION

Collection or generation methods:
  See the documentation folder listed under Processing summary. It records which plants
  merged into which, in which year, and the plant count per year.

Processing methods:
  See the documentation folder listed under Processing summary.

Standards and calibration:
  All geometries are held in CH1903+ / LV95, EPSG:2056.

Quality assurance procedures:
  - Coordinates were checked for a consistent coordinate reference system, reprojected
    where needed, and checked to fall within Switzerland.
  - The three layers of a given year were checked to hold an equal number of features.
  - Every merger or other change to a plant record was given a documented source.

Known limitations and uncertainty:
  - Coverage is uneven, and markedly so. Cantonal publication of wastewater treatment
    plant data through geodienste.ch is still being rolled out. Checked on 2026-08-10,
    11 of the 27 listed cantons and principalities were publishing the ARA topic
    (AR, BL, BS, LU, NW, SG, SH, SZ, UR, VD, ZG). The remainder were listed as
    "Im Aufbau", with announced availability dates spread from October 2026 to April
    2028, and four (AI, GL, NE, FL) with no date given. The amount and recency of
    evidence behind each plant therefore differs a great deal by canton, and the
    dataset is at its weakest where cantonal publication has not yet started.
    # This is the situation that motivated the dataset, and it will change. Re-check
    # the figures above when you next revise this readme.
  - Plant identifiers are not universally agreed. ARA_Nr sometimes differs between FOEN
    and the cantons. Where a divergence was found it is recorded in the Comment field and
    the alternative number is kept, but no single numbering is authoritative.
  - Reconstruction, not observation. Changes between years were reconstructed from
    heterogeneous sources rather than observed directly, so the year assigned to a merger
    or closure may be approximate.
  - Catchment boundaries carry more uncertainty than plant locations. A point can be
    verified against an address; a catchment polygon usually cannot.
  - Some plants discharge to no receiving water body, with the effluent seeping away
    instead. These are flagged in the Comment field of the discharge layer, and a
    discharge point may be absent or nominal.
  - [CONFIRM] whether any attributes were carried forward unchanged from ARA_2014 without
    re-verification. If so, saying which ones would help reusers greatly.
